Overview

PyPI recorded 185.0 billion downloads in July 2026, up from 176.7 billion in June — a nominal increase of 4.7%. Adjusting for the extra day (July has 31 days, June has 30), the true day-over-day average rose a more modest +1.4%, from 5.89B to 5.97B downloads per day. Growth has been consistent throughout 2026, continuing a trend that has seen monthly volume nearly double since February.

The macro picture remains stable: cloud infrastructure automation, AI toolchains, and data engineering continue to be the dominant drivers of PyPI traffic, with boto3 and the AWS SDK ecosystem alone accounting for roughly 2.8% of all downloads.

Geographic Distribution of Downloads

The United States retains a commanding lead with 140.4 billion downloads — approximately 75.9% of the global total. This level of concentration is consistent with prior months and primarily reflects US-hosted CI/CD pipelines, cloud build systems, and automated package resolution infrastructure rather than individual developer activity.

Notable Shifts

Singapore overtakes Germany for the #2 spot. Singapore climbed from 5.39B to 6.17B downloads (+14.5%), edging past Germany for the first time this year. Singapore's high ranking is largely driven by cloud egress traffic from AWS ap-southeast-1 and GCP asia-southeast1 regions. The continued expansion of cloud compute capacity in the region is likely the primary driver.

Sweden enters the top 15 for the first time. Sweden's download count jumped from 779M in June to 1.16B in July — a remarkable +48.9% surge. While part of this growth may be attributable to CI/CD infrastructure expansion in Nordic data centres, the magnitude of the jump is significant enough to warrant watching in August. China (CN), which held the #15 slot in June at 864M downloads, has dropped out of the visible top tier.

United Kingdom posts a sharp decline of −23.9%. UK downloads fell from 3.63B to 2.76B, the most notable negative shift among major markets this month. The UK has historically been driven by cloud-hosted build infrastructure in AWS eu-west-2, and a shift in workloads or routing patterns may be the underlying cause. This bears close monitoring — if it persists into August it may indicate a structural redistribution toward Ireland or other EU hubs.

Brazil continues its upward trajectory. Brazilian downloads crossed the 1B threshold for the first time, reaching 1.03B (+15.4%). This continues a multi-month trend of growth in Latin America, reflecting both a growing Python developer community and increasing adoption of automated pipelines in the region.

Key Observations in the Top 20

typing-extensions and cffi both grew ~20%. The typing-extensions library, a backport of Python's typing module for older interpreters, continues to expand rapidly as more libraries adopt PEP 695-style type annotations and tools like Pydantic, FastAPI, and mypy pull it in as a dependency. cffi growth (+20.2%) is likely correlated with the expansion of the cryptography package, which depends on it heavily.

setuptools is back on the rise. After a period of relative stability, setuptools jumped +15.5% to 1.56B. This may reflect a renewed wave of projects that still use setup.py-based builds and a wider adoption of pip in automated environment provisioning that implicitly pulls setuptools.

aiobotocore declined −7.5%. aiobotocore is the only package in the top 20 to see a meaningful drop. This may point to teams migrating async AWS workloads to newer async-native approaches or consolidating around the synchronous boto3 SDK in CI pipelines.

Emerging Packages

The following packages showed significant month-over-month growth in July, starting from a meaningful baseline (at least 100,000 downloads in June). These are not brand-new packages but ones gaining real traction, suggesting organic adoption curves rather than one-off spikes.

Note on methodology: Packages are considered "emerging" if they had ≥ 100,000 downloads in June 2026 and grew by more than 400% month-over-month in July, indicating accelerating community uptake rather than incidental CI noise.
gluonts +1,004% MoM
772K downloads in June → 8.52M in July
GluonTS is Amazon's open-source time series forecasting library, built on top of PyTorch and MXNet. The near-10× surge in July suggests it has been pulled into an automated dependency chain — likely a new default inclusion in a widely-used ML pipeline, data platform SDK, or SageMaker-adjacent toolchain update. Given the AI/ML space's continued investment in time series prediction for demand forecasting, anomaly detection, and infrastructure scaling, this growth trajectory appears sustainable.
uipath-langchain +836% MoM
945K downloads in June → 8.85M in July
The official LangChain integration package from UiPath, a leading RPA (robotic process automation) vendor. This growth reflects the ongoing fusion of traditional enterprise automation with LLM-based orchestration. As organisations deploy agentic workflows that combine structured RPA with unstructured reasoning, packages bridging these two worlds are expected to see strong adoption. The nearly 10× jump from a 6-figure baseline is a strong signal that UiPath's LangChain integrations are now shipping in production deployments.
xattr +863% MoM
1.82M downloads in June → 17.56M in July
xattr provides Python bindings for extended filesystem attributes on macOS and Linux. With an absolute volume of 17.5M downloads — already significant — this jump is striking. The most likely explanation is that a high-usage package added xattr as a new hard dependency, pulling it in across a broad range of environments. Packages in the file management, cloud sync, and development tooling categories are prime candidates. The absolute scale here distinguishes this from a mere statistical artefact.
nvidia-cuda-nvdisasm +1,088% MoM
120K downloads in June → 1.42M in July
A CUDA binary disassembler component from NVIDIA's ecosystem of Python-distributed CUDA wheels. While still relatively small in absolute terms, the 11× growth signals broader adoption of the CUDA-native Python toolchain — a trend driven by the explosion of GPU workloads for LLM training and inference. The NVIDIA CUDA Python packages (nvdisasm, nvcc, cublas, etc.) have been consistently climbing throughout 2026 as teams shift to managing CUDA installations entirely through pip rather than system packages.
dbt-core-experimental-parser +635% MoM
369K downloads in June → 2.71M in July
Part of the dbt (data build tool) ecosystem, this experimental parser component is likely being shipped as an opt-in dependency in newer dbt-core releases. The dbt ecosystem continues to grow rapidly as organisations invest in analytics engineering and data transformation pipelines. A 7× jump from a six-figure baseline suggests this parser module is now being bundled into common dbt installation workflows, indicating a shift from experimental to near-default status within the dbt community.
openhands +489% MoM
188K downloads in June → 1.11M in July
OpenHands (formerly OpenDevin) is an open-source AI coding agent platform that enables LLMs to write, execute, and debug code autonomously. Its crossing of the 1M monthly download threshold in July is a notable milestone and reflects the rising mainstream adoption of AI coding assistants beyond Copilot-style completions. Growth here is organic and developer-driven — OpenHands has an active GitHub community and is increasingly cited in AI engineering workflows where fully automated pull request generation and code review are desired.
fetch-use +4,624% MoM
142K downloads in June → 6.72M in July
The highest percentage growth in the dataset, fetch-use jumped from 142K to 6.7M in a single month. While the percentage is exceptional, the absolute volume (6.7M) is non-trivial and suggests genuine adoption. This package provides a declarative interface for HTTP fetch operations and appears to be gaining traction as a lightweight dependency in web scraping, API client, and test fixture frameworks. The growth pattern is consistent with a package being newly bundled as a default in a widely-used higher-level library.
